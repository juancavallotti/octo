Octo is open source under the [GNU Affero General Public License, version 3
(AGPL-3.0)](https://github.com/juancavallotti/octo/blob/main/LICENSE). In plain
language:

## You can run Octo in production, free of charge

Run Octo for your own projects or inside your company, commercially or not.
There is no fee, no seat count, and no obligation from running it. Development,
CI, staging, and production are all normal use.

## Your flows are yours

Building integrations on Octo is using the platform, not modifying it. Your flow
YAML, CEL expressions, resources, and templates are your business logic, not a
derivative work of Octo. You never have to open-source your flows, and
connecting Octo to your databases, APIs, and internal services creates no
obligations for those systems.

## What the AGPL asks of you

The AGPL applies to Octo itself: the runtime, the editor, the orchestrator, and
the rest of the platform code. If you modify that code and distribute it or run
it as a service others use over a network, you must make your modified source
available under the AGPL. The network clause distinguishes the AGPL from the
ordinary GPL: hosting a modified Octo for others counts as conveying it. If you
run an unmodified Octo, nothing is asked of you.

## Why AGPL

The license keeps improvements to the platform itself free for everyone. Anyone
can use, self-host, and build on Octo, but nobody can take it closed-source and
offer it as a proprietary hosted product.

## In practice

| Scenario | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Run Octo in production for your company | Allowed, no obligations |
| Build private integrations and flows | Allowed, your flows stay yours |
| Modify Octo and run it as a service | Share your modifications under AGPL |
| Offer a closed-source fork as a hosted product | Not allowed |

The full license text is at
[github.com/juancavallotti/octo/blob/main/LICENSE](https://github.com/juancavallotti/octo/blob/main/LICENSE).
